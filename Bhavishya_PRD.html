One-line product definition: Bhavishya is a multi-school digital platform that brings a student's school life into one secure parent-first experience—identity, attendance, academics, timetable, fees, transport, RFID events, cafeteria activity, notifications and structured parent communities.

1. Executive Summary

Bhavishya is designed as a unified school operating and parent-engagement platform rather than another attendance-only application. A school can operate its own isolated tenant inside Bhavishya, while parents receive a simple dashboard showing the information that matters to them about their child.

The prototype must demonstrate that the same platform can support multiple schools without mixing their data. A principal should see only their school. A teacher should see only the classes and students assigned to them. A parent should see only their linked child or children. A student should see their own academic and school information.

The product should feel like a real commercial product, not a hackathon dashboard: restrained colour usage, strong typography, generous whitespace, clear information hierarchy, meaningful empty states, realistic data and purposeful interactions.

2. Problem Statement

  • Parents often receive school information through multiple disconnected channels.
  • Attendance, fees, academic records, transport and school communications may live in separate systems.
  • Parents lack one simple place to understand what happened to their child during the school day.
  • School administrators need a consolidated operational view across students, teachers, attendance and payments.
  • Class-level parent communication can become noisy when handled through generic chat applications.
  • As schools grow, data isolation becomes critical: one school's students, teachers and records must never appear inside another school's tenant.

3. Product Vision

“A calmer way for schools and families to stay connected to a child's everyday school journey.”

Bhavishya should reduce uncertainty for parents while giving schools a structured digital operating layer.

4. Product Principles

  1. Parent-first: important information should be understandable within seconds.
  2. Useful over decorative: every card, chart and interaction must have a purpose.
  3. Calm visual system: no excessive gradients, glow effects, glassmorphism or “AI-generated dashboard” aesthetics.
  4. Human-made feel: deliberate spacing, typography, copywriting and interaction states.
  5. Privacy by design: role-based access and tenant isolation are core architecture requirements.
  6. Progressive disclosure: show the most important information first; details appear when requested.
  7. Realistic demo: demo data must behave like real school data, not random placeholder text.

7. Parent Experience

7.1 Parent Home

The home screen is the product's most important screen. It should answer, at a glance:

  • Is my child in school?
  • What class is happening now?
  • How is today's attendance?
  • Is there anything I need to act on?
  • Is the school bus on its route?
  • Are fees pending?

Recommended hierarchy: child selector → current school-day status → important alert → today's timeline → academics → fees → transport → cafeteria → community.

7.2 Child Timeline

A chronological school-day timeline can combine verified events:

  1. Bus boarded — 07:42
  2. School entry recorded — 08:18
  3. Period 1 — Mathematics
  4. Period 2 — Science
  5. Cafeteria purchase — 12:41
  6. Period 5 — English
  7. School exit recorded — 15:08

Every event must have a source/type and timestamp. Do not fake “live” events without clearly marking the prototype simulation.

7.3 Attendance

  • Today status: Present / Absent / Late / Not recorded.
  • Monthly calendar.
  • Attendance percentage.
  • Subject-level attendance where enabled.
  • Absence notification to the parent.
  • Principal can inspect school-wide attendance.

7.4 Academic Records

  • Exam list.
  • Subject-wise marks.
  • Percentage / grade where applicable.
  • Report card view.
  • Previous report cards.
  • Uploaded marksheets / documents.

7.5 Fees

  • Total fee amount.
  • Paid amount.
  • Pending amount.
  • Due date.
  • Payment history and receipt.
  • Clear CTA for online payment in production.

7.6 Transport

  • Assigned bus and route.
  • Bus status.
  • Last verified boarding event.
  • Estimated arrival information when real GPS is available.
  • Map screen for authorized parents only.

7.7 Cafeteria

  • Purchase date/time.
  • Item name.
  • Amount.
  • Daily/monthly spending summary.
  • Payment method / school wallet where applicable.

8. Parent Community

The community is deliberately not a WhatsApp replacement. It is a structured school communication layer.

Core concept

Each class can have a private community such as Class 8-A • Parent Community. Only authorized parents and school staff can participate.

Post types

  • Question
  • Announcement
  • Issue / Concern
  • Suggestion
  • Event / Activity
  • Poll

Professional behaviour

  • No uncontrolled direct messaging between all members by default.
  • Posts have categories, timestamps and status.
  • Issue posts can be marked Open → Under Review → Resolved.
  • School staff can respond officially.
  • Important announcements can be pinned.
  • Moderation and reporting tools are required for production.

9. Teacher Experience

  • Today's schedule.
  • Assigned classes and subjects.
  • Take attendance.
  • View class roster.
  • Record lesson topic.
  • View previous teaching logs.
  • See relevant parent/community questions.

Example teaching record: Class 8-A → Science → Topic: Force and Pressure → Period 3 → 10:20–11:00.

10. Principal / School Admin Experience

The principal dashboard should feel operational rather than overloaded.

  • Students present / absent today.
  • Teachers present / absent today.
  • Current school occupancy / activity summary where available.
  • Fee collection summary.
  • Recent alerts.
  • Transport overview.
  • Class-wise attendance.
  • Search student → open student dashboard.
  • Search teacher → open teacher dashboard.
  • Community issues requiring school action.

11. Student Experience

  • Personal profile.
  • School ID / digital ID.
  • Today's timetable.
  • Attendance.
  • Marks and report cards.
  • Assignments or academic notices if enabled.
  • Transport status where permitted.

12. RFID / Smart ID Architecture

The prototype should simulate RFID events rather than claiming actual hardware integration.

Production concept:

RFID Card / NFC / Smart ID
        ↓
Reader / Gate Device
        ↓
Secure Event API
        ↓
Event Processor
        ↓
Student + School Tenant Validation
        ↓
Attendance / Entry / Cafeteria / Transport Event
        ↓
Parent + School Dashboards

Every event should store school_id, student_id, event type, timestamp, device/source and verification status.

13. Transport Tracking

Production transport can integrate GPS-enabled school buses. The prototype can use simulated coordinates and a route timeline.

  • Bus ID.
  • Route ID.
  • Driver / assigned staff.
  • Current/last known location.
  • Route stops.
  • Boarding event.
  • Drop-off event.
  • Trip status.

Privacy requirement: precise child location must only be shown to authorized guardians and authorized school personnel, and retention should be limited.

14. Multi-School / Multi-Tenant Architecture

This is a critical requirement. Bhavishya must not be built as ten separate hard-coded projects. It should use one application with strict tenant isolation.

Bhavishya Platform
├── School A (tenant_id)
│   ├── Students
│   ├── Parents
│   ├── Teachers
│   ├── Classes
│   ├── Fees
│   ├── Attendance
│   └── Communities
├── School B (tenant_id)
│   └── ...
└── School J (tenant_id)
    └── ...

Every tenant-owned database record must carry a school/tenant identifier. Backend authorization must enforce the tenant boundary. Frontend filtering is never sufficient.

Tenant isolation rules

  • A parent from School A cannot query School B data.
  • A teacher from School A cannot open a School B class by changing a URL parameter.
  • A principal can only administer their assigned school.
  • Super Admin is the only role with cross-school administrative access.
  • All sensitive APIs validate both identity and tenant membership.
  • Audit logs record sensitive administrative actions.

15. Suggested Technical Architecture

Frontend

  • React + Vite or Next.js.
  • TypeScript.
  • Tailwind CSS or a disciplined CSS system.
  • Responsive design for desktop, tablet and mobile.
  • Component library built specifically for Bhavishya.

Backend

  • Node.js + TypeScript.
  • Express or NestJS.
  • REST API initially; event-driven services can be introduced later.
  • JWT/session-based authentication with secure refresh strategy.

Database

PostgreSQL is recommended for the core transactional system because students, parents, schools, classes, fees, attendance and relationships are strongly structured.

Supporting services

  • Object storage for report cards and documents.
  • Redis for caching / queues where needed.
  • WebSocket or Server-Sent Events for selected live dashboard updates.
  • Maps provider for production transport tracking.
  • Payment gateway for production fees.
  • Email/SMS/push provider for notifications.

18. Demo Dataset

The demo must be realistic enough for a live pitch but small enough to manage manually.

Recommended structure

  • 10 schools: School 01 through School 10.
  • 10 students per school: 100 students total.
  • 2–3 teachers per school: 20–30 teachers total.
  • At least 1 principal/admin per school.
  • At least 1 parent account per student.
  • Multiple classes and subjects per school.
  • Attendance history for the last 30 days.
  • At least 2–3 examinations with marks.
  • Fee invoices with paid, partially paid and pending states.
  • Sample RFID events.
  • Sample cafeteria purchases.
  • Sample bus routes and simulated GPS events.
  • Community posts with different statuses.

19. Important Demo Scenarios

  1. Parent logs in and sees their child's current status.
  2. Attendance changes from “Not recorded” to “Present” after a simulated RFID event.
  3. Parent receives an absence notification when the child is marked absent.
  4. Parent opens today's timeline and sees class/teacher/subject information.
  5. Parent opens a report card and views subject marks.
  6. Parent checks fee status and payment history.
  7. Parent opens transport and sees a simulated bus route on a map.
  8. Parent sees a cafeteria transaction.
  9. Parent posts a concern in the class community and sees status change to “Under Review”.
  10. Teacher logs in, opens a class and records attendance + lesson topic.
  11. Principal views school-wide attendance and teacher attendance.
  12. Principal searches a student and opens their student profile.
  13. Super Admin switches from School 01 to School 02 and demonstrates that data is isolated.

20. UX / UI Requirements

This is a primary success criterion for the prototype.

  • Professional institutional product, not a generic SaaS template.
  • Maximum 2–3 primary colours.
  • Strong neutral background and restrained accent colour.
  • No unnecessary neon, excessive gradients, glow, oversized blobs or decorative 3D graphics.
  • Use typography hierarchy rather than colour to create emphasis.
  • Cards should group meaningful information, not every tiny statistic.
  • Use real labels: “Attendance today”, “Fee due”, “Next class”, etc.
  • Mobile-first parent experience.
  • Desktop-optimised principal/teacher experience.
  • Every screen needs loading, empty, error and success states.
  • Tables should be readable and searchable.
  • Charts should answer a question rather than merely decorate the page.
  • Use subtle motion only when it improves feedback.

22. Security & Privacy

  • Passwords must be hashed using a modern password hashing algorithm.
  • Use secure authentication and authorization middleware.
  • Enforce school tenant boundaries server-side.
  • Validate all object ownership before returning records.
  • Do not expose sensitive identifiers unnecessarily in URLs.
  • Encrypt data in transit and use encrypted storage where appropriate.
  • Use audit logs for administrative changes.
  • Apply least-privilege access.
  • Protect report cards and personal documents with authorized, expiring access.
  • Do not expose precise child location to unauthorized users.
  • Define retention policies for location, RFID and transaction data.
  • Production deployment must be reviewed for applicable Indian privacy/data-protection obligations and school policies.

27. Demo / Pitch Story

The recommended live demonstration should follow one child's day instead of jumping randomly between features.

  1. Morning: child boards the bus → parent sees boarding event.
  2. Arrival: RFID entry → attendance becomes Present.
  3. School day: parent sees current class, subject and teacher.
  4. Lunch: cafeteria purchase appears in the timeline.
  5. Academics: parent opens marks/report card.
  6. Issue: a parent posts a structured concern in the class community.
  7. Afternoon: bus tracking shows the return journey.
  8. Administration: principal sees the school-wide attendance overview.
  9. Scale proof: switch school tenant and demonstrate completely separate data.

28. Acceptance Criteria

  • Users can log in according to their role.
  • Every school has isolated demo data.
  • Parent cannot access an unrelated student's records.
  • Teacher can view assigned classes and record attendance.
  • Principal can view school-level student and teacher attendance.
  • Parent can view attendance, academics, timetable, fees, transport and cafeteria records.
  • Attendance events generate appropriate notifications.
  • Community posts can be created and moved through an issue-resolution state.
  • Demo RFID, bus and cafeteria flows work end-to-end.
  • All major screens have loading, empty, error and success states.
  • UI works on mobile and desktop.
  • No feature relies on hard-coded UI-only data once the demo seed database is initialized.

29. Important Product Boundaries

  • Do not describe simulated GPS as real GPS.
  • Do not describe simulated RFID as physical RFID integration.
  • Do not claim Bhavishya is the first or only platform of its kind without market research.
  • Do not expose real children's personal information in a public demo.
  • Do not use production-looking credentials in a public repository.
  • Do not build the community as an unrestricted social network.

31. Seed Data Requirements for Opus / Implementation Agent

Create deterministic seed data so every demo run is reproducible.

  • 10 unique school tenants with realistic names, codes and academic years.
  • 10 students per school.
  • At least 2 parents linked to selected students to demonstrate multiple guardians.
  • 2–3 teachers per school.
  • At least 2 classes per school.
  • At least 4 subjects per school.
  • 30 days of attendance records.
  • 2–3 exams and subject marks.
  • Fee invoices across paid, partial and pending states.
  • Transport routes, buses and event history.
  • Cafeteria transaction history.
  • Notifications with read/unread states.
  • Community posts across Question, Announcement, Issue and Suggestion types.

32. Final Design Direction

Bhavishya should look like a serious education product.

Think: premium institutional software + excellent mobile banking clarity + modern education product usability. Avoid: flashy startup landing-page aesthetics, excessive cards, rainbow charts, neon gradients, fake 3D illustrations and generic AI-dashboard patterns.

The interface should make a parent feel: “I immediately understand what is happening with my child.”

33. Instruction for the Implementation / Design Agent

When using this PRD as the implementation brief, do not build a superficial static mockup. Build a functional prototype with a real database schema, seeded demo data, role-aware navigation, tenant-aware APIs, realistic state transitions and polished responsive UI.

Prioritize the quality of the parent dashboard and the multi-school architecture. The ten-school dataset exists to prove scalability and tenant separation, not to create ten different frontends.

Use reusable components and data-driven screens. A change to seeded data should automatically be reflected in the relevant dashboard. Avoid hard-coded statistics, names, attendance percentages, fee totals and timeline events in UI components.

34. Definition of Done

Bhavishya is considered pitch-ready when a reviewer can open the application, select a role, log in, understand the interface without explanation, follow one student's school-day journey, inspect the relevant records, interact with a parent community, open the principal dashboard and switch between at least two school tenants without seeing cross-school data.
